# Growth Playbook — the reach machine

Companion to `SALES_PLAYBOOK.md` (how to sell one), `COLD_CALL_KIT.md` (the
words), `DEMO_PRIYA_ELDER_CARE.md` (how to run a demo), and
`IMPLEMENTATION_GUIDE.md` (what happens after yes). This one is about volume:
turning one good demo into fifty.

**The decision this plan is built on (Arjun, 2026-07-30):** reach first, money
later. So every choice here optimizes for *number of places running the course*
and *referrals generated*, not for revenue. When money becomes the goal, revisit
— the likely path is charging for the human layer (kickoff workshop, machine
setup, staff training) around a course that stays free. Not now.

**North-star metric (put it on the wall):**
1. **Sites that have seen a live demo** — leading indicator.
2. **Sites running the course** — the real number.
3. **Referrals generated** — the compounding one. Track who referred whom.

Everything below is one of five parts of one machine: **find → refer → reach →
deliver → track.**

---

## Part 1 — The target universe (who, and where to find them)

Three audiences, all reachable, listed easiest-warmth first.

### A. Residential elder care — homes, assisted living, nursing, memory care
Where the list comes from, free and public:
- **State licensing directories.** Every US state publishes a searchable list of
  licensed facilities with address and bed count. This is the master list.
- **Medicare Care Compare** (nursing homes) — official, searchable by ZIP.
- **A Place for Mom / Caring.com / Google Maps** — "assisted living near me,"
  read the reviews (complaints about *activities* or *isolation* are your opener;
  staffing complaints are not).

### B. Senior communities — independent living, 55+ / active-adult, CCRCs
Same directories, plus local listings. These have activity calendars and a
life-enrichment lead just like homes do — same pitch, same champion.

### C. Senior social groups & programs (the underrated third)
Not buildings — gatherings. Often the *fastest* yes because there's no
procurement, just an organizer who wants good programming:
- **Area Agencies on Aging (AAA).** Every region has one. They coordinate and
  fund senior programs and know everyone — a single warm relationship here can
  seed a whole county.
- **Senior centers** (city Parks & Rec, National Council on Aging network).
- **Public libraries** with senior/digital-literacy programming.
- **Faith groups** with a senior ministry; **YMCA** senior programs; **OLLI**
  (Osher Lifelong Learning Institutes at universities); **Rotary / Lions**.
- **Meetup + Facebook Groups** — search "[your city] seniors / retirees / 55+."

### How to prioritize the list
1. **Local and drive-able first.** In-person is your edge; the demo converts far
   better in the room. Cluster by geography so you can do three in a day.
2. **Follow the network, not the map.** Activities directors know each other
   regionally — through state assisted-living associations and activity-pro
   groups (e.g. NAAP, the National Association of Activity Professionals). One
   champion vouching at a chapter meeting beats fifty cold emails.
3. **Warm always beats cold.** A referral from Priya jumps the queue over any
   directory name. Work referrals first, every time.

## Part 2 — The referral engine (your #1 lever — already running)

Priya forwarding you to other homes *is the business model for reach.* Cold
outreach only has to seed the first few in a region; referrals compound from
there. So systematize it:

- **Make the ask every single time.** At the end of every demo and every pilot
  kickoff: *"Who are two or three other homes, communities, or groups that
  should see this?"* Then stop talking.
- **Make saying yes effortless.** Hand them the ready-to-forward email (the one
  drafted for Priya — keep a copy in `COLD_CALL_KIT.md`). They don't have to
  write anything; they hit forward.
- **Close the loop.** When a referral converts, tell the referrer. People refer
  again when they see it mattered.
- **Log the chain.** In the tracker, record *source = referral + who*. The
  person who's referred you twice is your regional champion — invest there.

Rule of thumb: **one happy site should produce two named intros.** If it isn't,
the ask is missing or the demo didn't land — fix that before adding more cold
volume, because volume without referral doesn't compound.

## Part 3 — The outreach cadence (repeatable, not bespoke)

You can't hand-write an email per lead. Standardize to three templates (assemble
from `COLD_CALL_KIT.md`), personalize only the **first line**:

1. **Cold intro** — to a name off the list. First line names something real
   about them (their activity calendar, a review theme). Body is templated.
2. **Referral-forward** — the note a happy contact forwards (the Priya email).
3. **Post-demo follow-up** — same day, one thing they reacted to, a booking link.

**The sequence per cold lead** (then stop — don't nag):
- Day 0: cold intro email.
- Day 3: call / voicemail (the kit has the script).
- Day 7: one short "still worth 15 minutes?" email.
- No reply → nurture list, revisit in a quarter. Referrals skip straight to a
  warm first email.

**Kill the scheduling back-and-forth.** Put a free booking link (Calendly or
similar) in every email and voicemail. "Grab any 15-minute slot here." Email tag
to find a time is a silent bottleneck at volume.

## Part 4 — Delivery at volume (so demos and pilots don't cap you)

The product already helps: the course teaches, staff only supervise, and the
session sheets + machine checklist in `IMPLEMENTATION_GUIDE.md` mean a pilot
mostly runs itself. Add three multipliers:

- **One-to-many demos.** Present to *many* directors at once — pitch a slot at an
  activities-professional chapter meeting, or run a standing monthly "See it in
  15 minutes" video call that several leads join. This is the single biggest
  time multiplier you have.
- **A recorded demo.** A 3-minute screen recording (Loom) of the exact demo
  path. A lead who won't book can still watch it — and *forward* it. A recording
  scales to infinity; your calendar doesn't.
- **Train a champion per site.** Turn one staff member (usually the activities
  director) into the person who runs it. That site then needs **zero** of your
  ongoing time and becomes a reference + a referral source. This is how you hold
  50 sites without being in 50 buildings.

Your irreducible in-person time is: some first demos, and pilot kickoffs where a
champion can't be trained remotely. Everything else can be one-to-many or
recorded.

## Part 5 — Track it (a pipeline you'll actually keep)

Reach-first still needs a list so nothing falls through. Keep it stupid-simple.

**Stages:** To contact → Contacted → Demo booked → Demoed → Piloting → Running →
Referred onward.

**Columns:** Facility · Type (home / community / group) · Contact + role ·
Source (cold / referral — *and who*) · Stage · Next action + date · Notes.

**Tool:** a Google Sheet is enough to start. Move to Airtable or HubSpot-free
only when the sheet hurts. Don't buy a CRM before you have 50 rows.

The two numbers you review weekly: **demos done** and **sites running** — plus,
because it's the compounding one, **referrals generated this week.**

## The first 30 days (a concrete starting sprint)

**Week 1 — build the rails.**
- Pull 40 local targets from the state licensing directory + your AAA + libraries
  + two local senior Facebook groups. Put them in the tracker.
- Set up the booking link. Record the 3-minute demo video.
- Send Priya the forward-email; ask her for three names. Work those as the
  highest-priority warm leads.

**Week 2 — start the motion.**
- Send the cold intro to the first 20. Work Priya's referrals as warm intros
  first (they convert best). Book demos into the link.

**Week 3 — demo, and harvest referrals.**
- Run demos (in person where drive-able, video otherwise). End every one with
  the two-referrals ask and hand over the forward-email.

**Week 4 — deliver and compound.**
- Start one or two pilots. Follow up the rest. Find the next activities-pro
  chapter meeting and pitch a one-to-many slot. Review the three numbers.

Then repeat, with the referral list growing faster than the cold list each cycle.
That crossover — when most of your demos come from referrals, not directories —
is the machine working.

## The one rule that protects all of it

Money is later, so **the asset you're building now is a referral reputation** —
and it is fragile. One overclaim to one director poisons every intro they'd have
made. So the same discipline the sales docs demand applies double at volume:

- Never promise what isn't shipped — no dashboards, no per-learner reporting, no
  finished accessibility audit. (`SALES_PLAYBOOK.md §8` is the source of truth.)
- Never quote a scam statistic you haven't sourced that week.
- Lead with the two things that make people *want* to forward it: it's genuinely
  free, and it collects no personal data about anyone (only anonymous page-view
  totals). Those are why a director passes it
  to a peer without worrying it'll embarrass them. Protect that.
